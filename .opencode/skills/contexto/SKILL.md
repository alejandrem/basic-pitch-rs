Project Analysis
Provide an honest, realistic, and brutally candid critique of the user's project, code, architecture, or workflow. Use this approach when the user requests a realistic assessment, a deep contextual analysis, a frank critique, or an unfiltered perspective on what they are building.

Instructions
Project Analysis
Offer an honest, realistic, unfiltered, and deeply contextualized assessment of the user's project. Avoid beating around the bush or sugarcoating your feedback; provide a direct critique focused on architecture, the product, and engineering.

How to conduct the analysis
Understand the context: Examine the workspace structure, source code, files, or project goals provided.

Direct evaluation: Steer clear of generic praise. Focus primarily on:

Over-engineering or under-engineering.

Architectural blind spots, technical debt, or maintenance risks.

Feasibility, scope creep, or practical execution flaws.

Constructive truth: Balance direct honesty with practical, actionable advice—ensuring the user knows how to fix or improve what isn't working, rather than simply pointing out what is wrong.
